# Threats to Validity

Ranah Observatory must test for the following validity risks whenever they are relevant.

## Boundary changes
Historical observations may refer to different territorial units. Preserve source-era geography, version boundaries, and use explicit crosswalks.

## Definition and methodology breaks
Survey instruments, classifications, base years, sampling frames, and indicator definitions can change. Record known breaks and do not join incompatible series silently.

## Historical missingness
Older evidence is incomplete and uneven. Keep observed and reconstructed values separate, retain uncertainty, and never fill gaps merely to make a continuous chart.

## Measurement error
Administrative records, surveys, archives, remote sensing, and station data have different error structures. Preserve metadata and avoid false precision.

## Selection and survivorship bias
Observed regions, firms, events, or records may differ systematically from what is missing. Document how observations enter the dataset.

## Confounding and endogeneity
Development variables often influence each other. Regression coefficients, correlations, and feature importance do not by themselves identify causes.

## Post-treatment bias
Do not control or match on variables that are consequences of the exposure being studied. Define the causal timeline before selecting covariates.

## Temporal leakage
Future information must not enter historical prediction or evaluation. Use time-aware validation and versioned snapshots.

## Spatial dependence
Nearby regions can share markets, infrastructure, climate, watersheds, and shocks. Do not assume geographic observations are independent without checking.

## Peer-selection sensitivity
Claims of underperformance can depend on the comparison set. Document peer-selection rules and test plausible alternatives.

## Model misspecification
All statistical and ML models encode assumptions. Compare against simple baselines, inspect diagnostics, and test alternative specifications.

## Proxy validity
Indirect measures such as night-time lights or digital activity must be labeled as proxies and validated against direct measures where feasible.

## Disaster attribution
Hazard, exposure, vulnerability, observed impact, and attribution are different questions. Do not assign a flood, landslide, or loss to one driver without appropriate evidence.

## Historical-source bias
Archives and publications reflect the institutions that produced and preserved them. Triangulate material historical claims and preserve uncertainty or disagreement.

## External validity
Evidence from another province, decade, or institutional setting may not transfer directly to West Sumatra. Examine mechanism and context before extrapolation.

## Narrative bias
The project must remain able to reject its motivating hypothesis. Evidence that an apparent development gap is smaller, justified, or differently caused than expected is a valid result.
