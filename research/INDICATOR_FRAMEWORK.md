# Indicator Framework

The initial panel targets 40–60 carefully validated indicators across twelve domains:

1. demography and migration;
2. health;
3. education and knowledge;
4. labor and livelihoods;
5. income, productivity, poverty, and inequality;
6. agriculture, fisheries, forestry, industry, and trade;
7. infrastructure, transport, energy, and digital connectivity;
8. public finance and institutional capacity;
9. environment, land use, and climate;
10. disasters and resilience;
11. geography and market access;
12. historical and social context.

Core candidates include population, urbanization, migration, life expectancy, education attainment, labor-force participation, unemployment, underemployment, formal employment, real GRDP per capita, poverty, inequality, sector productivity, agricultural output and yield, manufacturing share, trade composition, investment, road access, transport activity, electricity, internet access, regional revenue/expenditure, rainfall extremes, temperature, land cover, disaster exposure/loss, elevation, slope, and travel-time-to-market measures.

Every indicator must define: `indicator_id`, name, domain, definition, unit, frequency, preferred geography, source priority, comparability notes, known breaks, and allowed claim type.

Availability alone is not sufficient. A core indicator must be interpretable, relevant to a research question, reproducibly sourced, and sufficiently comparable across time or regions.
