# Sumatera Barat construction: SE06 population and annual-survey boundary in 2006

## Why this matters

The annual construction-series checkpoint recovered a published Sumatera Barat count of **2,664*** construction establishments for 2006. A separate official BPS operation, the 2006 Economic Census (SE06), reports **4,504** Sumatera Barat construction establishments.

The SE06 publication now provides enough methodological context to establish that its listing is not merely another descriptive table. BPS explicitly describes SE06 as an operation used to identify establishment populations, update directories, and construct a complete **Master Sampling Frame** for economic surveys.

That strengthens the importance of the 4,504 benchmark while also making the non-equivalence with the annual-series 2,664 count more consequential.

## SE06 role and scope

`Sensus Ekonomi 2006 - Hasil Pendaftaran Perusahaan/Usaha - Sumatera Barat` (publication `05000.0733`, catalogue `9102005.13`) reports the May-June 2006 establishment listing using KBLI 2005.

The general explanation states that SE06 is intended to:

- identify establishment populations;
- update establishment directories;
- prepare an integrated establishment map/directory;
- obtain establishment populations by industry, business scale, and region;
- prepare a complete **master sampling frame** for subsequent economic surveys.

The listing covers category F, **Construction**, and explicitly includes businesses in permanent and non-permanent locations and businesses **with or without legal status**. The listing methods include door-to-door and snowball procedures.

This establishes SE06 as population/directory/master-frame infrastructure. It still does not establish that the complete SE06 construction listing is the exact operational frame used by the Annual Construction Survey.

## SE06 construction population

Table 12, `Number of Establishments by Industrial Classification and Scale of Enterprises`, reports for Sumatera Barat construction:

| SE06 scale | Permanent location |
|---|---:|
| Large | 20 |
| Medium | 110 |
| Small | 828 |
| Micro | 3,540 |
| Not classified | 6 |
| **Total** | **4,504** |

The same construction row reports zero non-permanent-location establishments. The five permanent-location components sum exactly to 4,504.

The total is independently reproduced in Table 14 and Table 1.c of the same BPS-authored publication.

## Legal-status decomposition

The same SE06 publication also separates permanent-location establishments by legal status.

Table 3 reports Sumatera Barat construction establishments **with legal status**:

- **1,379**

Table 4 reports construction establishments **without legal status**:

- **3,125**

These sum exactly to the complete construction listing:

`1,379 + 3,125 = 4,504`

Shares of the SE06 construction listing are:

- legal status: **30.617229%**
- without legal status: **69.382771%**

This legal-status decomposition is valuable because it tests a simple alternative interpretation of the annual construction-series count.

## Same-year comparison with the annual construction series

`Statistik Tahunan Perusahaan Konstruksi 2002-2006`, Table 4, reports Sumatera Barat 2006 as **2,664***.

The three same-year quantities are therefore:

- full SE06 construction population/listing: **4,504**
- SE06 legal-status-only construction population: **1,379**
- annual-series construction establishment count: **2,664***

The annual count is:

- **1,840 below** the full SE06 construction listing;
- **1,285 above** the SE06 legal-status-only population;
- **59.147425%** of the full SE06 construction listing.

It also does not equal the nonlegal-only population of 3,125.

## Negative identification result

This rules out several tempting shortcuts:

> `annual 2006 count != full SE06 construction population`

> `annual 2006 count != SE06 legal-status-only construction population`

> `annual 2006 count != SE06 nonlegal-status-only construction population`

Therefore a simple legal-status filter cannot explain the annual-series universe, and the annual count cannot be relabeled as the full SE06 population or master-frame count.

This is stronger than merely observing that 2,664 and 4,504 differ: the SE06 source explicitly documents its population and master-sampling-frame role, and its legal-status partitions still fail to reproduce the annual count.

## What remains unresolved

The evidence does **not** identify the exact operational annual-survey frame or the reason for the difference.

This checkpoint does not claim that:

- the annual survey excluded all micro establishments;
- the annual survey used legal status as a simple inclusion filter;
- the full SE06 listing was the exact Annual Construction Survey sampling frame;
- 2,664 was the 2006 annual-survey frame size;
- 2,435 was the 2005 annual-survey frame size;
- the end-2005 directory updating caused the same-year universe difference;
- the difference explains the historical 2001-2003 construction-value revision.

The exact annual-survey inclusion rules, directory/frame lineage, qualification strata, and period-specific expansion factors remain unrecovered.

## Consequence for the revision-mechanism hypothesis

The SE06 evidence adds a strong **negative identification constraint**. It confirms that BPS maintained population and master-frame infrastructure around this period, but the published annual-series establishment total does not map directly to the complete SE06 construction population or its legal-status partitions.

The mechanism candidate therefore remains operationally plausible but causally unproven. The next high-value evidence remains:

1. Sumatera Barat 2005 construction counts by qualification, preferably from `Profil Perusahaan Konstruksi di Luar Pulau Jawa 2005` (`05230.0610`) or an official reproduction; or
2. contemporaneous BPS documentation that explicitly identifies the construction directory/frame used by the Annual Construction Survey and its old/new counts or expansion factors.
