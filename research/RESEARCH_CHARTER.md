# Research Charter

## Project
**Ranah Observatory** is an open research infrastructure project for studying the long-run development trajectory of West Sumatra.

The project combines historical reconstruction, official statistics, geospatial data, environmental and disaster data, comparative regional analysis, econometrics, machine learning, and transparent policy scenario analysis.

## Core question
To what extent has West Sumatra converted its historical capabilities into broad-based development, what factors explain the gap between realized and attainable outcomes, and which interventions have the strongest evidence of improving future trajectories?

## Research questions

### RQ1 — Historical trajectory
How have population, health, education, livelihoods, productivity, infrastructure, environment, institutions, and resilience changed over time?

### RQ2 — Attainable development
Given geography, natural endowments, human capital, connectivity, infrastructure, and historical conditions, what outcomes are achieved by comparable regions?

### RQ3 — Divergence
When and where did West Sumatra diverge from comparable Indonesian regions, and in which dimensions?

### RQ4 — Explanatory factors
Which factors are strongly associated with divergence, and which relationships can be investigated with credible causal designs?

### RQ5 — Action
Which interventions appear highest-priority after considering expected impact, evidence strength, cost, implementation horizon, risk, resilience, and uncertainty?

## Scope

### Geography
- Primary: Province of West Sumatra and its regencies/cities.
- Comparative universe: Indonesian provinces and regencies/cities where comparable data are available.
- Spatial extensions: districts/villages, stations, watersheds, transport corridors, and raster/grid data where justified.

### Time
- Historical ambition: 1945 onward.
- Early period: archival and reconstructed evidence, with explicit uncertainty.
- Stronger quantitative period: approximately the 1960s onward as official statistical series become more systematic.
- Current period: annual, monthly, daily, event-level, or geospatial observations when useful.

### Boundary rule
Modern West Sumatra must not be projected backward as if administrative boundaries were unchanged. Historical observations require boundary metadata, a documented crosswalk, or a clear historical-region label.

## Development dimensions
The initial ontology covers:

1. demography and migration;
2. health;
3. education and knowledge;
4. labor and livelihoods;
5. income, productivity, poverty, and inequality;
6. agriculture, fisheries, forestry, industry, and trade;
7. infrastructure, transport, energy, and digital connectivity;
8. public finance and institutional capacity;
9. environment, land use, and climate;
10. disasters and resilience;
11. geography and market access;
12. historical and social context.

## Research principles

- Evidence before narrative.
- Provenance before visualization.
- Reproducibility before scale.
- Comparison before claims of underperformance.
- Prediction is not causation.
- Uncertainty must remain visible.
- Policy recommendations must distinguish evidence from assumptions.

## Public output taxonomy
Every analytical claim should be identifiable as one of:

- observed data;
- derived statistic;
- reconstructed historical estimate;
- predictive/model estimate;
- causal estimate;
- qualitative evidence;
- scenario assumption.

## Initial success criterion
The first milestone is not a polished dashboard. It is a reproducible research foundation containing:

- a canonical geography registry;
- a source catalog;
- an indicator framework;
- 40–60 high-value indicators with provenance;
- a comparative Indonesian panel where feasible;
- exploratory historical analysis;
- one baseline expected-performance/frontier model;
- one focused causal or quasi-causal case study;
- one climate/disaster case study relevant to West Sumatra.

## Non-goals for the initial phase

- claiming a single definitive monetary value for "wasted potential";
- using an LLM as a source of quantitative truth;
- building a large production web platform before the research layer is trustworthy;
- optimizing a policy objective without explicit assumptions and evidence;
- filling historical gaps merely to make charts continuous.
