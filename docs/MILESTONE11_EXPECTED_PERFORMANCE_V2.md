# Milestone 11 — Expected Performance Engine v2

## Purpose

Milestone 11 upgrades the M7 province-level proof-of-concept into a multi-outcome, cross-fitted expected-performance engine for the 19 current West Sumatra kabupaten/kota.

The engine asks a narrow predictive question:

> Given a geography's preregistered lagged structural profile and the common peer-year context, how different was the observed outcome from an out-of-geography conditional expectation?

It does not estimate a production frontier, causal effect, policy counterfactual, or monetary value of unrealized potential.

## Preregistered design

Model regime:

`sumbar_current_kabkota_lagged_structural_2019_2024_v1`

- 19 geographies;
- target years 2019–2024;
- 114 geography-year rows;
- three targets: poverty rate, unemployment rate, real GRDP growth;
- five predictors measured at `t-1`: mean years schooling, labor-force participation, agriculture share of GRDP, manufacturing share of GRDP, and rice yield;
- fixed ridge grid `[0, 0.01, 0.1, 1, 10, 100]`;
- target-year fixed effects;
- one preregistered sensitivity adds lagged CHIRPS annual rainfall.

The immutable pre-fit design snapshot remains in `data/manifests/milestone11_design_gate.json` with `model_fit=false` and `residuals_inspected=false`.

## Cross-fitting and validation

The primary estimates are generated by nested leave-one-geography-out validation.

For each outer fold:

1. one geography and all six of its target-year rows are removed from fitting;
2. ridge penalty selection is performed only inside the remaining 18-geography training universe using another geography-level CV loop;
3. the selected model is fit on those 18 geographies;
4. all six held-out rows are predicted.

Therefore all 342 primary predictions (`3 targets × 19 geographies × 6 years`) are out-of-geography.

The naive benchmark is the same-target-year mean among the other 18 geographies. A target qualifies only if the structural model beats this benchmark on both RMSE and MAE.

## Benchmark results

All three preregistered targets pass the locked benchmark gate.

| Target | Model RMSE | Naive RMSE | RMSE improvement | Model MAE | Naive MAE | MAE improvement |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Poverty rate | 1.959 | 2.550 | 23.16% | 1.364 | 1.728 | 21.06% |
| Unemployment rate | 1.714 | 2.039 | 15.91% | 1.218 | 1.355 | 10.10% |
| Real GRDP growth | 0.995 | 1.007 | 1.10% | 0.423 | 0.442 | 4.46% |

The growth model passes the pre-specified rule, but its advantage over the naive benchmark is small. M11 records the exact metric rather than promoting the three targets as equally informative.

## Regularization

Nested geography CV strongly favors regularization.

- poverty: all 19 outer folds select `λ=100`;
- unemployment: 17 outer folds select `λ=100`, 2 select `λ=10`;
- real GRDP growth: 13 outer folds select `λ=100`, 6 select `λ=10`.

This pattern is consistent with the small, correlated structural feature space. Coefficients remain model diagnostics only and are not causal feature effects.

## Support diagnostics

For every prediction, each lagged primary feature is compared with the 18 training geographies' min/max in the same lagged feature year.

Each target has 33 of 114 predictions with at least one marginal support warning. Those predictions remain in the output and are explicitly flagged; they are not deleted to improve benchmark metrics.

Marginal min/max support is not proof of multivariate overlap.

## Empirical uncertainty

For each focal geography and target, prediction intervals use cross-fitted residuals from the other 18 geographies only.

The focal geography's own residuals are excluded from calibration. The reported 2.5%–97.5% empirical intervals are exploratory finite-sample intervals and are not silently clipped to natural target bounds.

## Rainfall sensitivity

The preregistered sensitivity adds lagged CHIRPS annual rainfall to the five primary predictors.

- poverty: sensitivity slightly worsens RMSE and MAE;
- unemployment: sensitivity slightly improves both metrics;
- real GRDP growth: sensitivity slightly worsens both metrics.

The sensitivity can never replace the primary specification. CHIRPS remains `model_estimate` evidence and these predictive differences do not establish a causal rainfall effect.

## What the residuals mean

For benchmark-qualified targets:

`residual = observed - cross-fitted expected`

The sign has target-specific meaning:

- poverty/unemployment: a negative residual means the observed rate is lower than the conditional expectation;
- real GRDP growth: a positive residual means observed growth is higher than the conditional expectation.

These are predictive residuals. They are **not yet a development-gap decomposition** and M11 does not aggregate them into a single score or rupiah amount.

## Downstream implication

M11 provides three benchmark-qualified expected-performance signals, but it does not answer what the **attainable upper frontier** is. That distinction is reserved for Milestone 12.

M12 must compare frontier methods and cannot treat M11 conditional expectations as maximum attainable outcomes.
