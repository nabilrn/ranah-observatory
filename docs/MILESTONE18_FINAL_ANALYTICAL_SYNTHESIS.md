# Milestone 18 — Final Analytical Synthesis

## Result

**Phase 2 analytical-engine construction is complete.**

This does not mean the scientific research agenda is complete, and it does not mean Ranah Observatory is ready to rank real policies or publish a definitive monetary value for “wasted potential.”

M18 closes the analytical architecture by connecting the qualified evidence into a machine-readable graph while keeping uncertainty, method disagreement, failed identification attempts, and blocked claims visible.

## Final evidence graph

The graph contains nine synthesis nodes:

1. observed trajectory foundation;
2. expected performance;
3. attainable reference;
4. development gaps;
5. associated bottlenecks;
6. causal evidence;
7. spatial/climate constraints;
8. intervention scenarios;
9. uncertainty/evidence strength.

The graph contains 18 dependency, extension, readiness-constraint, and uncertainty-annotation edges. None of these dependency edges is labeled causal.

## What Phase 2 now supports

### Observed trajectory foundation

The current-boundary modern analytical regime contains:

- 19 West Sumatra kabupaten/kota;
- 2018–2025 analytical years;
- 15 integrated indicators;
- explicit missingness rather than imputation or temporal fill.

Historical evidence is much thinner. M6 contains one historical population anchor, seven modern series, and five trend-qualified modern series. General historical-boundary harmonization has not been performed.

Therefore the long-run 1945-onward research ambition remains open.

### Expected performance

M11 produces 342 geography-out cross-fitted predictions across poverty, unemployment, and real-GRDP growth.

All three target models beat the preregistered same-year peer-mean benchmark on both RMSE and MAE. This authorizes bounded predictive expected-performance interpretation.

It does not authorize causal interpretation of coefficients or residuals.

### Attainable reference

M12 calibrates favorable empirical references for the same three modeled outcomes using a primary conditional favorable-residual method and an alternative structural-neighbor favorable envelope.

The reference is empirical and conditional. It is not a theoretical maximum and not a guaranteed policy-attainable outcome.

### Development gaps

M13 contains 342 target-year-geography gap rows.

Relative to M11 empirical expected intervals:

- 15 rows are materially less favorable than expected;
- 313 are within the expected interval;
- 14 are materially more favorable than expected.

Gap interpretation is authorized for 243 rows and blocked for 99 where the pre-specified support rules do not authorize stronger interpretation.

The primary favorable-reference method and alternative structural-neighbor method disagree on the sign of the frontier gap in 50 rows. M18 keeps those disagreements visible rather than averaging them away.

No weighted multidimensional score or monetary aggregation is produced.

### Associated bottlenecks

M14 screens 11 preregistered lagged candidate/outcome-gap pairs and retains exactly one stable signal:

- lagged CHIRPS annual rainfall;
- unemployment adverse expected-performance gap;
- within-year rank association about +0.458;
- geography-block permutation two-sided p about 0.0056.

This is a stable association signal, not a causal bottleneck or policy priority.

### Causal evidence

M8 remains the completed 2009 West Sumatra earthquake quasi-causal study. The design passed its core identification diagnostics, but the result did **not** authorize a statistically robust directional nonzero differential-effect claim.

M15 adds explicit identification discipline:

- 1 completed quasi-causal study inherited;
- 2 `not_identification_ready` entries;
- 0 new causal models fit.

In particular, the M14 rainfall/unemployment association is not promoted to a causal effect merely because it was stable.

### Spatial and climate constraints

M16 integrates qualified hazard/climate/recorded-occurrence evidence while preserving component semantics.

It has four substantive inherited evidence objects and eight blocked/gap objects. Compatible qualified exposure, vulnerability, capacity, and observed-impact components remain incomplete for full disaster-risk synthesis.

Therefore:

- `risk_synthesis_authorized = false`;
- no composite disaster-risk score exists;
- recorded flood/landslide event counts are not relabeled as observed impact.

### Intervention scenarios

M17 contains seven scenario contracts:

- five quantitative predictive model-state sensitivities;
- two blocked intervention scenarios;
- 15 feature-target mappings using all 19 M11 outer folds.

The common perturbation is symmetric `±0.5` training-fold standardized units. Results are predictive model sensitivities, not treatment effects.

Method instability remains visible. The least sign-stable mapping is agriculture share of GRDP → real-GRDP growth, with dominant-sign retention `10/19 ≈ 0.526` across outer-fold models.

M17 still has:

- no causal policy counterfactual;
- no forecast authorization;
- no qualified implementation cost;
- no estimated implementation horizon;
- no cost-benefit ranking.

## Claims that remain explicitly blocked

M18 publishes nine `not_authorized` claim boundaries. They include:

- a definitive monetary value of West Sumatra's “wasted potential”;
- treating the favorable empirical reference as a theoretical maximum;
- treating M11 predictive residuals as causal underperformance;
- treating M12 favorable-reference distance as a guaranteed policy gain;
- treating the M14 rainfall association as a causal unemployment effect;
- treating BNPB recorded event counts as observed disaster impact;
- treating the M16 component frame as a composite disaster-risk score;
- treating M17 predictive sensitivities as policy treatment effects or forecasts;
- ranking M17 scenarios by policy attractiveness or cost-benefit without qualified cost/horizon/effect evidence.

The machine-readable claim-boundary ledger records what additional evidence would be required to upgrade each claim.

## What Phase 2 completion means

Phase 2 now has a reproducible analytical chain:

`qualified evidence -> modern analytical panel -> cross-fitted expectation -> empirical favorable reference -> multidimensional gaps -> stable association screening -> causal/identification library -> spatial/climate component constraints -> scenario sensitivity -> final evidence-strength synthesis`

This is the stable analytical substrate required before a later delivery/dashboard layer can safely expose conclusions to users.

## What remains scientifically open

The largest evidence-expansion priorities are now explicit rather than hidden:

1. **Historical reconstruction** — add table-qualified historical series and defensible boundary semantics toward the 1945-onward ambition.
2. **Long-run comparative divergence** — build longer comparable regional panels capable of identifying when and where divergence occurred.
3. **Causal identification** — find genuinely new natural experiments or independent temporal/spatial variation for priority mechanisms.
4. **Disaster-risk chain** — qualify compatible exposure, vulnerability, capacity, and observed-impact evidence with explicit methodology/vintage.
5. **Action evidence** — connect intervention-specific causal effects to feasible raw-unit changes, costs, implementation horizons, risks, and uncertainty before any policy ranking.
